Privacy and cookies

Last updated: 26 November 2026. What we collect, why, and what you can ask us to do with it. We never sell your data.

Who we are

The Wild Learning Collective is run by Matthias Clarke as a sole trader. Matthias is the data controller for the information described here. You can reach us at hello@thewildlearningcollective.com.

What we collect

When you buy something: your name, email, delivery address and order details, plus the book you choose. Payments are handled by Stripe. We never see or store your card number.

When you sign up for the free Thinking Pack: your email address, so we can send you the pack and occasional news about Little Fox. You can unsubscribe at any time.

When you send a group or school enquiry: your name, email, school or group, role, year group, roughly how many children, and anything else you choose to tell us.

When you ask about a reading with Olivia: your name, email, type of event, town or venue, preferred date, number and ages of children, and any notes.

When you email us: whatever you include in your message.

How we use it

  • To take payment for and deliver your order, and to send order and delivery updates
  • To run the Club and Christmas pre-orders, including refunds
  • To reply to enquiries and arrange readings
  • To send the Thinking Pack and news, if you signed up
  • To keep the records the law requires

We use your information to fulfil our contract with you, because you consented (for email news), or because we have a legitimate interest in replying to your enquiry. We don’t use automated decision-making or profiling.

Who we share it with

Only the services we need to run the business:

  • Stripe: payments (stripe.com/gb/privacy)
  • Formspree: receives the forms on our website and passes them to us by email (formspree.io)
  • Royal Mail or a tracked courier: your name and address, to deliver your order
  • GitHub Pages: hosts our website; like all web hosts, it briefly processes visitors’ IP addresses to serve the pages
  • Google Fonts: supplies the typefaces on our pages, so your browser contacts Google’s servers when a page loads
  • Plausible Analytics: counts visits anonymously (see Cookies below)

Some of these companies are based outside the UK. Where data leaves the UK, they protect it with approved safeguards such as the UK International Data Transfer Addendum.

How long we keep it

Order records: six years, as HMRC requires. Email sign-ups: until you unsubscribe. Enquiries: up to twelve months, unless we’re working together.

Cookies

We don’t use advertising or tracking cookies, so we don’t need to ask for your consent.

  • Visitor statistics: we use Plausible, which counts visits without cookies and without collecting personal data. It doesn’t follow you across other websites.
  • Activities saved on your device: if you type answers into the reflection sheet, they’re saved in your own browser so they’re still there when you come back. They never leave your device or reach us, and “Clear our answers” removes them.
  • Checkout: Stripe may set its own cookies during payment to prevent fraud. These are covered by Stripe’s privacy policy.

If we ever add cookies that need your consent, we’ll ask first and update this page.

Children and the Wild Challenge

Our books are for children, but our website and checkout are for adults. We don’t knowingly collect information from anyone under 16. If you think a child has sent us their details, contact us and we’ll delete them.

The Wild Challenge invites families to post what they find on their own Instagram and tag us. Those posts are public and covered by Instagram’s terms. We ask grown-ups to share what their child found rather than photos of their child, and we never publish children’s faces on our website.

Your rights

You can ask us to show you, correct, delete or hand over the information we hold about you, object to how we use it, or withdraw consent for emails at any time. Email hello@thewildlearningcollective.com and we’ll reply within one month.

If you’re unhappy with how we’ve handled your data, you can complain to the Information Commissioner’s Office at ico.org.uk.

Changes

If we change this policy, we’ll update the date at the top of this page.
